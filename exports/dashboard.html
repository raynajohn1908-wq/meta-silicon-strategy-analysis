Product conclusion

Total capex is not the right standalone success metric for MTIA. Public evidence supports workload-level efficiency and a broader portfolio strategy. The better product test is whether Meta can route workloads to the best available hardware with strong unit economics, power efficiency, reliability, supply flexibility, and manageable software friction.

Product decision framework

These are the decision dimensions an infrastructure product team would need to weigh. Public disclosures support the importance of several of them, but Meta does not publish enough data to score every dimension externally.

Workload fit

Match the accelerator to training or inference needs, model requirements, latency, and throughput.

Performance / dollar

Compare useful throughput delivered per unit of cost.

Performance / watt

Measure how efficiently each path uses constrained power and data-center capacity.

Software portability

Estimate the engineering work required to move workloads across hardware paths.

Developer velocity

Evaluate whether the infrastructure reduces hardware-specific friction for model teams.

Reliability & maturity

Determine whether the full hardware and software path is production-ready at Meta scale.

Capacity & supply

Assess resilience to vendor concentration, constrained supply, and rapid demand growth.

Total cost of ownership

Capture deployment, operation, support, and infrastructure costs by workload.

Why software portability is part of the product

A heterogeneous hardware portfolio only creates value if teams can use it without excessive hardware-specific engineering work.

What public data still cannot answer

A precise MTIA ROI would require internal variables that Meta does not publicly disclose.

Missing variableWhy it matters
Like-for-like accelerator TCONeeded to quantify unit-cost savings on comparable workloads.
Silicon spending or deployed-compute mixNeeded to measure how much of the portfolio is shifting toward custom silicon.
Migration engineering effortNeeded to quantify software portability and developer-velocity costs.
Demand-normalized infrastructure costNeeded to separate efficiency gains from rapid growth in AI workload volume and complexity.

Bottom line

Public evidence supports MTIA as an efficiency, flexibility, and diversification strategy. It does not prove that MTIA lowers Meta's total infrastructure bill. The product value of heterogeneous compute depends on both hardware economics and the software and systems layer that makes the hardware usable.
